# Public-interest use cases

Redtail Open Verifier is intended to support public-interest workflows where digital records may later be disputed, altered, removed, or challenged.

These are intended use cases, not current deployments or validated users.

## 1. Public record integrity

A journalist, researcher, archivist, or civil-society organization publishes a public record, dataset, document, image, or supporting material and wants others to verify later that the record still matches the originally published proof.

The verifier does not prove that the contents are true. It only helps detect whether the record differs from a previously published integrity proof.

## 2. Evidence-supporting materials

An organization working on accountability, transparency, or human-rights documentation may need to preserve supporting materials such as images, documents, metadata, datasets, or archived references.

Redtail Open Verifier can provide a reproducible way to check whether those materials still match their recorded hashes and proof data.

## 3. Independent archival verification

Researchers or archivists may want to preserve records outside a single hosted platform.

A self-hostable verifier and portable verification receipts can help make record checks possible without depending on Redtail, a hosted endpoint, or one operator.

## 4. Tamper-evident change history

A record may change over time. The relevant question is not only what the record says now, but whether its change history is internally consistent.

The verifier is intended to help check continuity and consistency of a record history, not the legal or factual validity of the record contents.

## Current status

The project currently has no validated users, adopters, deployments, or partnerships in these public-interest communities. Outreach and usability testing with public-interest workflows are planned future work.
